Auto-tracking laser turret

It finds the fly.
And it keeps the dot on it.

A benchtop turret that spots a small flying insect with stereo cameras, confirms it by wing-beat frequency, predicts where it's headed, and steers a laser to stay locked on — all inside a hard safety interlock. Built as a Frankenstein of proven tech: the Photonic Fence architecture, an open-source galvo aiming chain, and the optical-wingbeat ID literature.

How it works

01

Detect

Stereo IMX219 cameras + IR flood pull the insect out against a matte backstop.

02

Track

A constant-velocity Kalman filter locks one target and predicts its next position.

03

Confirm

An FFT wing-beat gate checks for a 100–700 Hz signature — not dust, glints, or a hand.

04

Aim

A learned 3D→DAC map drives the two-axis galvo to the predicted lead point.

05

Gate

The interlock permits the beam only when every safety condition is true.

Specifications

Sensing
2× Raspberry Pi Camera v2 (IMX219) stereo pair, 6 cm baseline, 850 nm IR flood + matte backstop
Target ID
IR photodiode + FFT wing-beat gate — confirms flies, fruit flies, gnats by their ~100–700 Hz wing-beat
Compute
NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano — tiny-YOLO detection at 30+ FPS, 60 Hz control loop
Beam steering
Two-axis galvanometer via MCP4922 12-bit DAC; calibration fit residual ~1.6 DAC counts
Tracking
6-state Kalman filter with lead-point prediction to cancel sense→aim latency
Safety
Latching e-stop · backstop-required · size + wing-beat gate · key & enclosure interlock
Laser
Your choice, added last — bring the whole system up on a <1 mW pointer first

Safety first — not optional

  • A steered beam points in unpredictable directions. Anything over ~1 mW is an eye hazard the moment it's uncovered.
  • The turret will not fire without a solid, in-range backstop behind the target — the beam never leaves the box toward a face or window.
  • Oversized or wrong-cadence targets (a hand, a pet) hard-inhibit firing.
  • Build the hardware interlock first; the software gate is defence-in-depth on top of it, never instead of it.
  • Run a real high-power diode only inside a closed, interlocked enclosure, wearing wavelength-matched goggles.
What it is: a supervised DIY engineering project and a working software stack.
What it isn't: a finished product, and not harmless — you supply and own the laser.
